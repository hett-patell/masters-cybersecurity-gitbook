# Ground truth

!!! warning
    What real people say online — with the caveat that Reddit is not outcome data.


=== "🇬🇧 UK"

    > _"Getting a job in the UK as an international student is impossible."_ — r/UniUK ⚠️

    > _"Your salary will most likely range from 25–35k for your 1st year."_ — r/cybersecurity ⚠️

    **Pattern:** consistent reports that international graduates struggle to find Skilled Worker sponsors, especially at the salary threshold. Cybersecurity is not on the Shortage Occupation List.

=== "🇨🇦 Canada"

    > _"Applied for over 200 jobs but nothing came back. Pay is lower — 75k."_ — r/cscareerquestions ⚠️

    **Pattern:** competition is fierce in the Canadian tech market generally, but cybersecurity is a growth area. The 3-year open PGWP removes the permission bottleneck that kills UK prospects.

=== "🇳🇿 New Zealand"

    Limited Reddit data. The market is very small. Most posts discuss the general difficulty of breaking into any tech role in NZ as a new graduate.

    **However:** the Green List mechanism means you don't need to win a competition — you need to land one qualifying role. The path is binary, not probabilistic.


---

## How to weight this

| Source type | Use it for | Don't use it for |
|---|---|---|
| **Reddit** | Identifying patterns, red flags, structural barriers | Salary estimates, employment rates |
| **University marketing** | Programme content, teaching approach | Graduate outcomes, salary claims |
| **Immigration rules** | Visa eligibility, timelines, thresholds | Job market conditions |
| **HESA / Stats NZ / StatCan** | ✅ Actual outcome data — but not found for cyber-specific programmes | — |

!!! info
    The settlement case in this book compares **immigration mechanisms**, not whether you'll actually get hired. That's the honest limitation.
